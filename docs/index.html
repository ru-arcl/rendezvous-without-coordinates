Rendezvous without Coordinates

Each agent sees only which side of its windshield its target left from, and turns that way. With a narrow enough windshield, they all meet.

How it works

Each agent is a Dubins car: it moves forward at a constant speed and can only go straight or turn left or right at rate ω. Its only sensor reports whether its assigned target is in the left half of the windshield, the right half, or out of view. When the target slips out of view, the agent turns toward the side it left from; when the target is back, it drives straight.

The sum of distances to targets, V, is a Lyapunov function: for a cycle of n agents it is guaranteed to decrease when φ is below the bound (π/n for n ≥ 5). Above π/n a regular polygon can grow forever. With sampled control the threshold is higher, about π/n + ωΔt/2, and moves toward π/n as ωΔt shrinks.

The green mark on the φ slider is the paper's guarantee that V decreases for the current graph and speeds (Theorems 4, 12, 13 and Lemma 11); the blue mark is π/n. For an intree with different speeds the paper only guarantees merging (Lemma 14, φ < π/2), so no mark is shown. Keys: space plays or pauses, s steps, r resets.
